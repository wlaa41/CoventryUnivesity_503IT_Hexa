# Cybersecurity Questions for Teenagers (Ages 13–17)

**Topic focus:** Social media privacy, phishing, friend hacking, reputation, scams, two-factor authentication.

---

**1. The Hacked Friend**

*Your friend "Jake" DMs you on Instagram: "Hey, I accidentally reported your account. Click here to appeal before it's deleted." The link looks like Instagram.*

**What should you do?**

- A) Click the link – you don't want to lose your account.
- B) Message Jake on a different app to confirm.
- C) Ask the link to be sent again.

**Answer: B** – Hacked accounts are common. Verify urgent requests through another channel.

---

**2. The Public Story**

*You post a photo of your new school ID badge on your public story. It shows your full name, photo, and school logo.*

**What's the risk?**

- A) None – it's just school info.
- B) Strangers can learn your name, school, and possibly location.
- C) The school might be angry.

**Answer: B** – Never post ID cards, tickets, or boarding passes publicly.

---

**3. The Free Spotify Premium**

*A TikTok video claims: "Free Spotify Premium for 1 year – just enter your email and password here." The website looks real.*

**What should you do?**

- A) Use a spare password you don't care about.
- B) Report the video – it's a phishing scam.
- C) Try it – you can always change your password.

**Answer: B** – "Free" offers that ask for login credentials are almost always scams.

---

**4. The Old Photo Tag**

*You're tagged in an embarrassing photo from three years ago. Someone comments, "Send me $10 or I'll share this to the whole school."*

**What should you do?**

- A) Pay $10 – it's cheap to avoid embarrassment.
- B) Screenshot everything, block the user, and tell a trusted adult.
- C) Ignore it – they won't really share.

**Answer: B** – This is blackmail. Never pay. Document and report.

---

**5. The "Your iCloud Is Full" Email**

*You get an email that looks exactly like Apple: "Your iCloud is 99% full. Verify your account now or lose data." It asks for your Apple ID and password.*

**What's the safest action?**

- A) Click the link and log in – it looks real.
- B) Open your iCloud settings directly (not via the email) to check.
- C) Reply to the email asking if it's a scam.

**Answer: B** – Always go directly to the official app or website. Never click links in suspicious emails.

---

**6. The Two-Factor Code Request**

*A friend messages you on Discord: "I'm trying to log into my account but I accidentally sent the 2FA code to your phone. Can you read it to me?"*

**What should you do?**

- A) Give the code – it's your friend.
- B) Call your friend to see if it's really them.
- C) Ignore the message.

**Answer: B** – Scammers hack accounts and then ask friends for 2FA codes. Verify by voice or video.

---

**7. The Location Sharing**

*A new person in your class asks to share live locations on Snapchat so you can "hang out after school." You've only known them for two days.*

**What's the best choice?**

- A) Share it – they seem nice.
- B) Wait until you know them much better in real life.
- C) Share but only for one hour.

**Answer: B** – Never share live location with people you don't fully trust offline.

---

**8. The Leaked Password List**

*You read that a game you play had a data breach. Your email and hashed password might be leaked.*

**What should you do immediately?**

- A) Nothing – the password was hashed.
- B) Change your password on that game and any other site using the same password.
- C) Delete the game.

**Answer: B** – Use unique passwords per site. After a breach, change it everywhere it was reused.

---

**9. The "Send This to 10 People" Chain**

*A message says: "If you don't forward this to 10 friends, your account will be hacked tonight."*

**What should you do?**

- A) Forward it – just to be safe.
- B) Delete it – this is a hoax designed to spread fear and spam.
- C) Send it only to 5 friends.

**Answer: B** – Chain messages about hacking are almost always fake.

---

**10. The Job Offer DM**

*An unknown account offers you a "social media assistant" job: $300/week just to post links. They need your full name, address, and bank details for payment.*

**What should you do?**

- A) Give fake details.
- B) Report and block – legitimate jobs don't ask for bank info via DM.
- C) Ask for an interview first.

**Answer: B** – This is a common scam to steal identity or money.

---

**11. The VPN Promo**

*A YouTuber you trust promotes a free VPN saying, "It makes you completely anonymous and un-hackable."*

**What should you think?**

- A) The YouTuber wouldn't lie.
- B) Free VPNs often sell your data. No tool makes you 100% unhackable.
- C) Install it – it's better than nothing.

**Answer: B** – Be critical of influencer endorsements. Free VPNs have risks.

---

**12. The Private Story Leak**

*You add a new person to your private story. Next day, screenshots appear in a group chat. You're humiliated.*

**What's the lesson?**

- A) Don't use private stories at all.
- B) Only add people you completely trust in real life, and assume anything digital can be shared.
- C) Report the person and move on.

**Answer: B** – Digital trust is fragile. Screenshots can be taken by anyone.

---

**13. The QR Code on a Poster**

*You see a poster at a bus stop: "Scan to win a free phone." You scan the QR code and it opens a website asking for your phone number and email.*

**What's the danger?**

- A) None – QR codes are safe.
- B) It could lead to a phishing site or download malware.
- C) Only dangerous if you enter payment info.

**Answer: B** – QR codes can hide malicious links. Be cautious of random ones in public.

---

**14. The "Your Photo Is Being Used" DM**

*A stranger messages: "Someone is using your photos on a fake account. Click here to see and report it." The link looks odd.*

**What should you do?**

- A) Click – you want to protect your photos.
- B) Search for your name on the platform yourself, don't click the link.
- C) Ask the stranger for screenshots first.

**Answer: B** – Scammers use fear to drive clicks. Always investigate directly.

---

**15. The Group Chat Bully**

*Someone creates a fake anonymous account and posts mean things about you in a large group chat. No one knows who it is.*

**What is the most effective first step?**

- A) Respond angrily to defend yourself.
- B) Screenshot everything, leave the chat, and report to the platform and a school counsellor.
- C) Create a fake account to fight back.

**Answer: B** – Don't engage. Collect evidence and report.
